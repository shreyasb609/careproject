# Research & Background: Post-Hospital Transitions of Care

## The Problem: The "Discharge Cliff"

Hospital discharge is one of the most vulnerable points in healthcare delivery. Studies published in peer-reviewed clinical literature (e.g. *Annals of Internal Medicine*, *The Joint Commission Journal on Quality and Patient Safety*, and *JAMA Internal Medicine*) consistently report that:

1. **High Readmission Rates:** Nearly 1 in 5 Medicare patients discharged from a hospital are readmitted within 30 days, many due to preventable adverse drug events and missed follow-up appointments.
2. **Medication Discrepancies:** More than 50% of discharged patients experience at least one medication discrepancy between their pre-admission regimen and their discharge orders.
3. **Low Health Literacy:** Over 40% of patients cannot accurately state their post-discharge diagnosis or identify which medications were added, continued, or stopped.
4. **Caregiver Burden:** Informal caregivers (family members and friends) frequently take on complex medical management with little to no formal training or organized handoff materials.

## Evidence-Based Solutions Integrated into CareTransition

- **Clear Medication Reconciliation Timetables:** Presenting medications by time of day (Morning, Noon, Evening, Bedtime) with unambiguous discontinued status reduces accidental duplication.
- **Demystifying Error-Prone Abbreviations:** The Joint Commission and ISMP advocate eliminating error-prone abbreviations (e.g., *QD*, *QOD*, *U*). CareTransition translates these into plain language and alerts users to potential ambiguity.
- **Structured Caregiver Handoff:** Structured handoffs (similar to hospital nursing shift reports) significantly reduce miscommunication during transitions to home care.
- **Low-Cognitive Burden Interface:** Calming colors, minimal clutter, large readable controls, and mobile accessibility support patients recovering from anesthesia, surgery, or serious acute illnesses.
